Accountability for AI trading bots

Every trading bot has to keep its receipts.

AI agents now trade real money in milliseconds. RouteClerk makes an agent write down what a trade will cost before it makes it, then checks the real result afterward. If the bot's numbers don't add up, it is paused until someone explains why.

Your money stays in your own account the whole time. RouteClerk can't hold or move funds — it can only stop a bot from opening its next position.

In plain English

The five words that matter.

You don't need any trading knowledge. Here is every piece of jargon on this page, translated.

Basis point (bps)
One hundredth of a percent (0.01%). So 2.3 bps is about 0.023% — a tiny slice of the trade's value.
Mid price
The midpoint between the best buy and best sell price. The fair "reference" price the bot is judged against.
Commitment
The bot's signed promise — its chosen route and the most a trade should cost. It is locked in before the trade, so it can't be faked after.
Autopsy
The signed cost report written after the trade, comparing the promise with the real result — the "receipt".
The gate
The rule that pauses a bot whose costs were too far off, until the gap is explained or covered. The only power RouteClerk has.
Custody
Who holds the money. It always stays with you — RouteClerk never holds or withdraws funds.
How it works

Four steps, every trade.

STEP 01

Promise the trade

The bot locks in which exchange it will use and the most the trade should cost, with a deadline. The signed promise can't be altered afterward.

STEP 02

Make the trade

The order runs through Elysium's fast order path on your own exchange account. Your money never leaves your control.

STEP 03

Check the receipt

RouteClerk compares the real cost with the promised cost — the fill price against the fair mid price, plus fees and funding.

STEP 04

Continue or pause

Small gaps pass and the bot trades on. Too big a gap pauses the bot until the mismatch is explained or covered with a bond.

Under the hood

Four functions do the whole job.

For developers: the on-chain gate is deliberately tiny. An order path asks one question before it fires, and the answer lives on-chain.

contracts/RouteClerkGate.sol 
// 1. the bot promises a route and a cost
submitCommitment(id, routeHash, expectedCostBps, sizeUsd, deadline)

// 2. the order path asks one question before it acts
canAct(agent)  // true unless a failed autopsy is open

// 3. the clerk files the receipt after the trade
submitAutopsy(id, commitmentId, realizedCostBps, fundingDriftBps, thresholdBps)

// 4. unblock: explain, or cover the gap
acknowledgeAutopsy(id)  /  coverDeviation(id)  payable
